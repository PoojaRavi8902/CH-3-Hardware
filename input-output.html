3.2 Input and Output Devices

Comprehensive study of hardware interfaces and the Physical-Digital bridge.

3.2.1 Scanning Technologies

Devices like 2D Scanners use a bright light and a Charge-Coupled Device (CCD) to convert reflected light into electrical signals. 3D Scanners use lasers or X-rays to measure the X, Y, and Z coordinates of an object to build a digital CAD model.

Barcodes vs QR Codes

  • Barcodes: 1D linear codes read by laser/LED. Primarily used for stock identification in retail.
  • QR Codes: 2D matrix codes read by a camera. Can store much more data (URLs, contact info).

3.2.2 Sensors and Control Systems

In automated systems, sensors measure physical properties (analogue). For a computer to understand this, an ADC (Analogue to Digital Converter) is required.

The Process:
  1. Sensor sends analogue signal to ADC.
  2. ADC sends digital data to the Microprocessor.
  3. Microprocessor compares data to a Pre-set Value.
  4. If action is needed, a signal is sent to an Actuator (e.g., motor, heater).

3.2.3 Output Technologies

Inkjet Printers use liquid ink droplets (Thermal Bubble or Piezoelectric), while Laser Printers use dry powder (Toner) and static electricity to bond images to paper using a fuser.

Touchscreen Technologies

Type How it Works Pros/Cons
Capacitive Detects change in electric field from a finger. ✅ Durable, supports multi-touch. ❌ No gloves.
Resistive Physical layers press together to complete a circuit. ✅ Cheap, works with stylus/gloves. ❌ Easily damaged.

LCD & LED Displays

Most modern screens are LCD. They require a backlight. LED-backlit screens use LEDs which reach maximum brightness instantly and consume less power. OLED screens are self-lit and don't need a backlight at all.
